Pipeline Steps

Email ingestion through CRM record creation
1
Email Received Trigger
An inbound email containing an RFP (Request for Proposal) arrives from a potential NRG energy customer. This is the entry point for the pipeline.
2
Extract Data FRED
FRED reads the email and extracts structured proposal and customer data from unstructured text — customer name, contact info, energy requirements, pricing details, and other RFP fields.
  • Customer and contact information
  • Energy proposal details
  • Pricing and volume data
  • Any additional RFP fields
3
Generate Review UI FRED
FRED generates a structured interface displaying the extracted customer and proposal data for human review. Presents the information in a clear, editable format.
4
Review & Approve Human
A sales rep reviews the extracted data for accuracy and completeness. This is the human-in-the-loop gate — the rep confirms FRED got it right before anything enters Salesforce.
5
Sync to Salesforce Automated
Approved data is automatically pushed to Salesforce as a new record. Manual data entry is eliminated — the rep's approval is the only required action.

Reference

Context and design notes
A What FRED Does

FRED is an internal NRG AI agent that automates RFP intake. Potential energy customers send RFP emails; FRED extracts the relevant data, surfaces it for human review, and pushes approved records to Salesforce.

The core problem FRED solves: sales reps were manually reading inbound RFP emails and re-entering the information into Salesforce. FRED removes that step.

The human stays in the loop for quality control — FRED handles extraction and entry, the rep handles judgment.
B Workflow vs. Agent

FRED's pipeline is largely deterministic: email in → extract → UI → approve → Salesforce. The steps don't change based on what FRED finds.

The extraction step (step 2) is where AI earns its place — reading unstructured email prose and inferring structured fields is a reasoning task, not a template match. Whether FRED qualifies as a "true agent" depends on whether it makes autonomous decisions about how to handle novel or incomplete RFPs.

C Duplicate Customer Entries

Salesforce uses email address as the primary deduplication key — when FRED pushes a record, Salesforce checks whether a Contact with that email already exists. If yes, it updates; if no, it creates a new record.

This creates a known issue: duplicate customer entries appear whenever the email FRED extracts doesn't match the one already in Salesforce.

ScenarioWhat happens
Same customer, different sender address Work email vs. alias or personal email → Salesforce sees two people
FRED extracts the wrong email CC'd colleague or reply-to header grabbed instead of customer's address
No email in the RFP body Record lands in Salesforce without a dedup anchor
Forwarded RFP "From:" is an internal forwarder — every forward looks like a new sender
The human review step (step 4) is supposed to catch this — but if reps are checking proposal data and not the email field, duplicates slip through.
D Teams
